AIApp

Lucy

A role-aware assistant that answers a project team’s how-do-I questions by searching the team’s own approved guidance and showing where each answer came from.

Quick Search for acronyms, contacts, tools, links and templates.
Deep chat that blends keyword and meaning-based search, boosted by the asker’s department, segment and role.
A “Do I need approval?” check that names the required approver before you act.
How the AI works
Model
Sentence-transformer embeddings in ChromaDB and FAISS, plus BM25 keyword search, fused as 0.45 BM25 + 0.35 FAISS + 0.20 Chroma. No text-generating model: it retrieves, it does not write.
Input
A question, who is asking (department, segment, role) and the team’s guidance documents.
Output
Cited result cards with source and snippet, assembled from fixed templates.
Guardrails
Deterministic ranking so the same question gives the same answer, role-based visibility, and approval rules kept in a reviewable file.

AIApp

SympliLyfe

A personal life operating system that brings habits, budget, health, news, events and language learning into one app, with a companion character named Aelio.

The Hearth: a daily briefing that combines weather, calendar, habits and fitness goals into one summary.
Budget, habits, health and language learning in one app, with more than 4,200 verified vocabulary entries.
A summoned radial navigation wheel with eight stops in place of fixed tabs. Live on the web.
How the AI works
Model
OpenAI gpt-4o-mini, locked to an allowlist so no route can call a more expensive model.
Input
Context the user already keeps in the app: habits, calendar events, forecast and goals.
Output
A feature-specific answer the app renders: briefing, habit plan, fitness plan, budget tips or news summary.
Guardrails
Content moderation, per-user and global daily spend caps, and a fallback answer when a model call fails.

Non-AIApp

Atlas

A language-learning world where you pick a place, not a lesson. A market stall teaches a greeting and a dish, the study room practises what you just heard, and each evening adds a star to your own constellation. Shown here as a working prototype.

A hub with five wings: Study, Culture & Cities, Food & Mercado, Events, and Volunteer & Give Back.
The Mercado: four stalls (Spanish, Italian, Japanese, Arabic), each with three phrases spoken aloud by the browser and the dish behind them.
“A day in the Atlas”: a seven-step guided tour from morning briefing to evening reflection, plus “Your Atlas”, a constellation that gains one star a day.
How it works
Stack
One self-contained HTML file: plain JavaScript, scene artwork, browser speech for the phrases, progress saved in the browser. No server.
Approach
Language lives inside places. Aelio, the guide, offers one place, one phrase and one thing to practise each day. A missed day leaves a gap in the sky, not a reset.
Status
An interactive prototype of the experience, with seeded content such as the local events. In SympliLyfe, the Atlas stop is still a dev-preview doorway.
Next
Build the scene player in the app, then split Atlas out as its own app with voice, motion and activity features.

Non-AITool

Folder Reader + Diff

A browser tool that compares two folders and shows what was added, removed, changed or moved, with a side-by-side diff. It runs entirely in the page and sends nothing anywhere.

Load two folders and every file is marked Same, Diff, Only A or Only B, with moved and renamed files detected.
Pick one file on each side for a side-by-side or inline diff and a similarity score on content, path and SHA-256.
Export JSON and CSV reports, bundle folders into one file, and ignore files with gitignore-style rules.
How it works
Stack
Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No server, no dependencies.
Approach
Pairs files by relative path, hashes them with SHA-256 in the browser, then scores similarity for the pair you select.
Key feature
Moved and renamed detection, plus Pack Merge to combine many folders into one de-duplicated bundle.

AITraining deck

I can explain it, not just use it

A self-study deck I wrote on enterprise AI architecture: how language models work, how APIs and tool use work, retrieval, agents and MCP, and what a deployment needs around the model.

246 slides across eight modules, with formula sheets, a glossary, scenario drills and a final exam.
Every formula is worked by hand with numbers: softmax and temperature, BM25, chunk counts, compounding errors, caching cost.
Figures are dated and sourced to Anthropic and MCP documentation. About 11.5 hours for a careful first pass.
Reading list
Attention Is All You Need (Vaswani et al., 2017). The paper that introduced the Transformer, the architecture behind today’s language models.
Agentic Large Language Models (van Duijn, van der Meer, Plaat and van Stein, 2026). A free textbook (CC0) on how language models take actions and work together as agents.
Introduction to AI Safety, Ethics and Society (Dan Hendrycks, 2024). Readable online in full, with an audiobook on Spotify.
